Merchandising forecast basics for analysts

Retail analytics · Forecasting

A merchandising forecast answers one question: how many units should we buy, in which sizes and colours, and when? Three measures do most of the work.

Sell-through

Sell-through is units sold divided by units received, usually over a set period. A style that sells 60 percent of its receipts in the first six weeks is performing. Track it by style, colour and channel, not only at total level.

Weeks of cover

Weeks of cover is current stock divided by average weekly sales. If you hold 800 units and sell 100 a week, you have 8 weeks. Compare it against the lead time from order to shelf. Cover shorter than lead time means a stock-out is already in motion.

Forecast accuracy

Measure the gap between forecast and actual sales at the level you buy. Weighted absolute percentage error is simple to explain to a buying team. Review it monthly and fix the styles that miss consistently, rather than averaging the errors away.

A simple buy calculation

Buy quantity = (Forecast units for lead time + safety stock) - Stock on hand - Open orders

Keep the safety stock as an explicit input that buyers can see and change. Hidden buffers are where excess stock hides.

What to check before you present

Confirm that the sales data excludes returns or includes them consistently, that the calendar aligns with trading weeks, and that new styles are forecast from comparable launches rather than from zero.
