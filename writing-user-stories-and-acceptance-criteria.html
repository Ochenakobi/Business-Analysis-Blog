Writing user stories and acceptance criteria that developers can build from

Business analysis · Agile

A user story is a short statement of who needs what and why. Acceptance criteria turn that statement into something a tester can check.

The story format

Use "As a [role], I want [capability], so that [benefit]." The benefit matters. It tells the team which trade-offs are acceptable when time runs short.

Write acceptance criteria as examples

Use the Given, When, Then pattern. For example: given a buyer has a purchase order over USD 10,000, when they submit it, then the order routes to a second approver. Each criterion should describe one behaviour and one result.

Keep stories small

If a story needs more than a few criteria, split it. A good test is whether the team can finish it within one sprint and demonstrate it to a user.

Review with the people who will test

Read each story with a developer and a tester before the sprint starts. Most gaps appear in the first five minutes of that conversation.
