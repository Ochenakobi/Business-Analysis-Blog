How to write a BRD for an ERP rollout

Business analysis · Requirements

An ERP business requirements document (BRD) is the agreement everyone signs before anyone configures a system. A good one is short enough to read and specific enough to test against.

1. Start with the business problem, not the system

Write two or three paragraphs on what the business does today, what hurts, and what success looks like in numbers. For example: "Month-end close takes 9 working days; target is 4." If the goal cannot be measured, the BRD will drift.

2. Define scope in one table

In scopeOut of scopeOwner
Purchase-to-pay for 3 factoriesRetail POS integrationFinance lead
Vendor master data migrationHistoric data before 2022IT lead

Keep the out-of-scope column honest. Most change requests start there.

3. Write requirements as testable statements

Use the form "The system shall..." with one behaviour per line and a unique ID. "The system shall allow a buyer to approve purchase orders up to USD 10,000 without a second approval" can be tested. "The system should be user-friendly" cannot.

4. Capture current process and future process

Map both, and note every change in who does what. Roles that vanish in the future state are where resistance and missed tasks usually come from.

5. List assumptions, dependencies and open questions

Each open question needs an owner and a due date. A BRD with ten open questions and owners is more useful than one that pretends everything is settled.

6. Agree sign-off criteria before you start

Write the acceptance tests now, in plain language, and attach them to the requirements they check. This is the cheapest point to agree what "done" means.

A practical structure: purpose, scope, current state, future state, requirements with IDs, reports, data, controls, assumptions, open questions, sign-off.
